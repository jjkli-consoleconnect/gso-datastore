Level Description SLA
Reference Information:
A Service Level Agreement SLA) for a network operations center NOC) ticketing system is
based on the priority or severity of the ticket. The recommended SLA for all tickets is to
define different SLAs for different priority levels to ensure that critical issues are addressed
more quickly than minor requests. Here are the key metrics you should track and the
recommended approach for setting SLAs for each:
Key SLA Metrics
First Response Time ⏱️
This is the time it takes for an agent to make the first public reply on a ticket after it's been
submitted. A fast first response is crucial for setting customer expectations and
demonstrating that the issue has been acknowledged.
Priority 1 Critical): Immediate or within 1530 minutes, often with a 24/7 schedule. This is
for a major service outage affecting a large number of users.
Priority 2 Major: Within 12 hours, during business hours. This is for a service
degradation or an issue affecting a smaller group of users.
Priority 3 Minor: Within 4 hours, during business hours. This is for a minor issue or a
request for information.
Priority 4 Notice/Low): Within 1 business day. This is for routine changes or non-urgent
requests.
Resolution Time 🚀
This is the total time from when the ticket is created until it's fully resolved. The timer should
be paused when the ticket is in a "Pending" state, such as when you're waiting for a
response from the customer or a third-party vendor.
Priority 1 Critical): Within 4 hours, on a 24/7 schedule.
Priority 2 Major: Within 9 hours, during business hours.
CE request to add Ports / Device to Console Connect.
Ad-hoc request fulfillment
Priority 3 Minor: Within 18 hours, during business hours.
Priority 4 Notice/Low): Within 45 hours, during business hours.
Agent Work Time
This metric measures the total combined time an agent spends working on the ticket while
it's in a New or Open status. It excludes time when the ticket is on hold (e.g., waiting for
customer feedback). This is a good way to track your team's efficiency on a per-ticket basis.
The specific targets for this metric will vary widely depending on the complexity of the
tickets, but you can set a goal for how much time an agent should spend actively working on
a ticket before escalating it or moving it to a different status.
Important Considerations
Ticket Priority: The most effective SLAs are based on a priority matrix that combines the
impact of the issue with its urgency. For example, a single user's printer not working is
low impact, but a CEO's printer not working is high impact.
Business Hours vs. Calendar Hours: Define whether your SLAs will run on a 24/7 basis
(calendar hours) or only during your team's normal business hours. This is especially
important for lower-priority tickets.
Automation: Use your ticketing system's automation features to set alerts for when an
SLA is about to be breached. This helps agents prioritize their work and prevents overdue
tickets.
Measurement: To ensure your SLAs are effective, you need to have a clear way to
measure them. Most modern ticketing systems can automatically track these metrics, but
you should ensure your definitions for "first response" and "resolution" align with your
business needs.
Delayes
Most common:
Waiting for another team to come back to us or to update us.
This can take less than 24 hours or a few days at a time.
Waiting for the customer to get back to us or Support.
This can take less than 24 hours or a few days at a time.
Waiting for a PM or GSDT to complete an installation.
This can take weeks or months at a time.
How to over come the delays:
Perform regular check up or follow ups on linked tickets or directly to the responsible
party.
Monitor the case on a daily or weekly basis.
General Guideline
. Check the CCAS Queue for new cases
a. KPI:
i. Within less than 30 Minutes since a new ticket was logged in the CCAS queue.
ii. A new follow up from the Dev team or Support should be acknowledged or attended
to within 20 min from the update.
iii. New requests should be action-ed if it is directed to Application Support or Support
ask the Dev team for help.
iv. All new requests that has relevance should be raised on the SE Call with Lloyd
. Monitor the automated notifications channel
a. KPI:
i. With all new failures on the notifications channel an acknowledgement should take
place in less than 20 min.
ii. An update to what happened should be less than 1 hour.
iii. Cases that are held back by an issue:
. For follow up actions see the Business as usual KPI under manage customer
centric issues.
. Manage customer centric issues.
a. KPI:
i. All new customer centric issues should be logged with Application Support and into a
CCAS.
ii. The new cases need to be raised on the SE call if its not answered and closed.
iii. For all CCAS cases that is customer centric we need to take action even if the Jira is
closed.
iv. Depending on the severity of the case the response should be:
. Business as usual:
a. within 20 min.
b. Follow up within 4 hours.
c. Follow up within 2nd day, each day.
. Escalation/Emergency:
a. within 20 min.
b. Follow up within 2 hours.
c. Follow up within 1 day, each day.
d. Raise to Manager as soon as possible.
v. If no response:
. There is no need to make continues follow-ups for weeks and months if there are
no reply.

open the Jira if further help is required".
